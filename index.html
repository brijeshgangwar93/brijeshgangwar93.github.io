About

Photonics, IIT Bombay — nanofabrication to nonlinear optics

I'm a PhD researcher in the Department of Physics at IIT Bombay, working in the Laboratory of Optics of Quantum Materials (LOQM) under Prof. Anshuman Kumar. My work sits at the intersection of nanofabrication and nanophotonics — building optical structures small enough that the shape of the device changes the physics of the light inside it.

Two threads run through my research: whispering-gallery-mode microresonators, where light circulates around a cavity edge much like sound around the dome of a whispering gallery, and bound states in the continuum on photonic metasurfaces, a way of trapping light that in principle never leaks out. I'm also interested in how fabrication choices — ion-beam polishing, 2D material integration — determine how close real devices get to their theoretical limits.

In 2022 I was awarded the Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF) for this work. I'm a member of Optica (formerly OSA) and the IEEE Photonics Society, and I've served with the OSA–IEEE Photonics Student Chapter at IIT Bombay.

Research

Two directions, one question: how far can confined light be pushed?
Direction A

Whispering-gallery modes in microresonators

Light circulating at the rim of a microdisk builds up intensity through total internal reflection, enabling strong nonlinear effects at low input power. Sidewall roughness is the main loss channel — my work uses focused-ion-beam polishing on SiO₂ microdisks (5–20 µm diameter) to push quality factors closer to their theoretical ceiling.

MicroresonatorsFIB fabricationNonlinear optics
Direction B

Bound states in the continuum in photonic metasurfaces

BICs are modes that coexist with a continuum of radiating states yet remain, in the ideal case, perfectly confined — giving metasurfaces with very high Q despite being open, planar structures. I work on realizing and probing these states, including in 2D-material and hBN-integrated platforms for sensing and single-photon emission.
